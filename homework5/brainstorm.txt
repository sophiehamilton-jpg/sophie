2. Resource 1: https://medium.com/@bharatambati/simulating-the-n-body-problem-a-way-to-understand-celestial-objects-f5de281f12cd
    This article explains the equations needed to simulate gravitational interactions between multiple massive objects. When a system has more than
    two bodies, it is difficult to predict paths of motion as Kepler's Law of Universal Gravitation cannot be used. The article provides an 
    equation derived from Newton's second law which can be integrated to determine gravitational interactions.
Resource 2: https://ckrao.wordpress.com/2021/12/31/calculating-the-locations-of-stars-in-the-sky/
    This article outlines how to calculate the position of a star as a function of declination, time, and observer latitude. It provides a 
    derivation for a fuction which states that vector A'P = cos(2(pi)t) A'B + sin(2(pi)t) A'C, in which A' is the location of the star, P is
    your position on Earth, B is the star's highest location, and C is the position the star will be after a quarter of a day
Resource 3: https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~sdalessi/EJP2023.pdf
    This study from the University of Waterloo explains double pendulum motion. It describes techniques that can be used to model and understand
    double pendulum kinematics. Double pendulums are chaotic systems with motion that is difficult to predict. The paper walks through derivations 
    for kinematic equations which can be used to describe these pendulums.
